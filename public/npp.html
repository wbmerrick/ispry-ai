Notice of Privacy Practices

Your health information.

This notice describes how medical information about you may be used and disclosed, and how you can get access to this information. Please review it carefully.

Effective October 12, 2026 · William B. Merrick, DO, PLLC (iSpry)

Who this covers

The practice keeps your chart in Akute. Membership and payment records are kept in Stripe. You may start an iSpry website account (name, email, password, age, sex, Tennessee) before chatting with Spry. Spry, the intake assistant on this website, prepares education and a summary. That summary is filed to Akute when you become a member or finish intake and submit contact details. The chat itself is not your chart.

How the practice uses and shares information

  • Treatment: to review your intake, see you by telehealth, and carry out a plan.
  • Payment: to bill a one-time visit or the membership through Stripe, and to know whether a membership is active. A one-time visit payment is not a membership.
  • Operations: to run the practice, including scheduling, quality review, and security of the record.

Other uses, including most marketing and any sale of health information, need your written permission. You may revoke that permission in writing later. The practice may also share information when the law requires it, such as certain public-health reports or a valid court order.

Your rights

  • See or get a copy of your chart.
  • Ask for a correction if something is wrong.
  • Ask for a list of certain disclosures.
  • Ask the practice to limit what it shares, and ask for confidential communication, such as a specific phone number.
  • Get a paper copy of this notice.
  • Name someone to act for you, where the law allows it.

Ask for any of these by calling the practice. The practice may say no to a request when the law allows it, and will tell you when it does.

The practice’s duties

The practice must keep health information private, give you this notice, and follow it. If the notice changes, the new notice will be posted on this page and will apply to information the practice already has, as the law allows.

Complaints

You may complain to the practice or to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. The practice will not retaliate against you for filing a complaint. HHS accepts complaints at hhs.gov/hipaa/filing-a-complaint.
